DoorDash Mission-First Home

A prototype that reframes DoorDash from a storefront browser into a mission orchestrator. Instead of asking hosts to stitch together five apps and five carts, it captures mission intent up front and assembles one curated multi-merchant checkout.

The user story

Maya wants to host Galentine's. What should be joyful becomes logistics fatigue: wine in one app, flowers in another, substitutions somewhere else, and checkout fragmentation everywhere.

The product bet

Mission intent should be a first-class input. Order data tells what users buy; mission data tells why. That why-signal powers better orchestration and repeat trust.

What we built in the MVE

The MVP starts at the home screen with mission chips (Dinner, Brunch, Game Day, Cocktail Night, Casual Gathering). Users select mission type, confirm guest count and date, then review a curated multi-merchant cart that supports fast edits and a single checkout path.

  • Mission-first entry point above the standard store grid (non-blocking and skippable).
  • Rules-based template assembly (ops-authored), not ML or concierge.
  • Inline substitution and fee transparency before checkout confirmation.
  • Structured analytics events for declaration, completion, abandonment, and repeat mission behavior.

Why this matters

This is not just a UI tweak; it is a platform positioning shift. The feature is designed as the intake layer for Mission Intelligence: without mission declaration, downstream systems (templates, cart quality learning, mission memory) have no reliable signal to optimize.

Primary metric

Mission declaration rate among hosting-eligible users.

The earliest product-controlled funnel lever and the leading indicator for everything else.

Business expectation

Scenario modeling from the team deck estimates meaningful incremental GOV and retention upside as declaration and repeat behavior scale.

Framed as ranges, not single-point promises.
